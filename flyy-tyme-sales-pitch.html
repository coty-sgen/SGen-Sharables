03Three things to surface — gently

These are openings, not gotchas. Spot them. Don't lecture.

Flag 01

FLYY TIME vs FLYY TYME

The two decks spell the brand differently. FLYY TIME in the standalone deck, FLYY TYME everywhere else (including the partnership name and 444 Invitational). Ask which is locked. Whichever it is, every URL, logo, social handle has to match.

This is a free "you guys clearly think about the small stuff" moment for you.

Flag 02

Two target audiences don't fully line up

FLYY TIME deck says 28-45, premium spirits/cigars/rooftop crowd, "Distinguished Hedonist," lounge culture. SADDBERRYS/FLYY TYME deck says 21-40, fashion-conscious, music/nightlife/creative communities.

Overlapping but not the same buyer. Ask: "Is FLYY TYME the elder lounge brand and SADDBERRYS the product that pulls in the younger fashion crowd? Or are you threading one audience?" Their answer tells you whether to build one site or two.

Flag 03

Cannabis + paid search reality

Google Ads bans direct cannabis promotion. So does Meta on most placements (Instagram is more workable for lifestyle-adjacent posts that don't directly sell flower). Their growth plan needs to lean organic SEO, content, influencers, events — not paid Google search.

Worth saying out loud so they don't budget for what won't work.

06Smart questions to ask

In rough order. Use them to qualify and to spec the build mid-meeting.

  1. "Is it FLYY TIME or FLYY TYME?" — non-negotiable. Gets the consistency conversation going.
  2. "Are FLYY TYME and SADDBERRYS one site or two?" — drives the package recommendation.
  3. "Are you cultivating, licensing, or partnering on the flower?" — Nevada is tight; this changes what compliance pages they need.
  4. "Where are you on the Invitational — booked venue, sponsors signed, ticketing plan?" — opens the door to a funnel + event microsite upsell.
  5. "What's your current spend on the sponsorship side, and is it landing?" — opens the door to Meta Ads + content + SEO recurring.
  6. "What's your fundraise timeline?" — if they're closing in 60 days, that's the urgency. They need us to ship fast.
  7. "Who's currently building the website?" — tells you if you're displacing someone or filling a vacuum.
  8. "Have you reserved the matching domain(s)?" — sometimes the answer is no, and that's a quick value-add you handle on the spot.

07How to open — 90 seconds

Riff, don't read.

Your opener

"I read both pitch decks last night. You're not building a cannabis brand — you're building a fashion brand that happens to sell flower. That changes everything about how the site needs to feel. Most agencies will give you a dispensary template. That's not what you need.

Before I get into what we'd build, two things: one, I want to confirm the spelling — FLYY TIME or FLYY TYME, because I saw both in your decks. Two, are FLYY TYME and SADDBERRYS one website with sections, or two separate sites talking to each other? Your answer changes my recommendation."

Then shut up and let them talk. They'll fill in 80% of what you need to spec the build.

08The close

Don't quote a number cold. Walk them through Tier B as "what I'd build for a brand at your stage." Let them flinch or not, then say:

Your close

"We can phase this. Start with the FLYY TYME hub and SADDBERRYS product site — that's the investor-grade core. The Invitational event microsite and the merch + upsell layer can ship after we know the round is closing.

Let me put together a written scope this week. What's the best email?"

Get the meeting after the meeting. Don't try to close on the spot for a Custom-tier build.
